FSSAI Schedule 4 - Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations

4.1 General Hygiene Requirements
All food premises must be maintained in a clean and sanitary condition at all times.
Food preparation surfaces must be cleaned and sanitized daily using approved food-grade sanitizers.
Floors, walls, and ceilings must be kept clean and in good repair.

4.2 Personal Hygiene
All food handlers must wash hands thoroughly with soap before handling food, after using the toilet, and after handling raw food.
Food handlers must wear clean protective clothing including aprons, gloves, and head coverings.
Personnel with communicable diseases must not handle food.

4.3 Temperature Control
Perishable food must be stored at or below 5 degrees Celsius.
Hot food must be maintained at or above 60 degrees Celsius during service.
Food must not be kept in the temperature danger zone (5-60 degrees Celsius) for more than 2 hours.

4.4 Pest Control
Food premises must be kept free from pests including rodents, insects, and birds.
A documented pest control program must be maintained and records kept for inspection.
Pest control treatments must be carried out by licensed operators.

4.5 Waste Management
Food waste must be removed from food preparation areas regularly and at least once daily.
Waste bins must have tight-fitting lids and be cleaned regularly.
Waste storage areas must be separate from food storage and preparation areas.

4.6 Water Supply
Only potable water from an approved source must be used in food preparation.
Water storage tanks must be cleaned and disinfected at least once every six months.

4.7 Equipment and Utensils
All equipment and utensils in contact with food must be made of food-grade materials.
Equipment must be cleaned and sanitized after each use.
Cutting boards for raw meat and ready-to-eat food must be kept separate.

HACCP Principles
Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) must be implemented in all food businesses.
Critical control points must be identified, monitored, and documented.
Corrective actions must be taken and recorded whenever a CCP is out of control.
All HACCP records must be retained for a minimum of 2 years.
